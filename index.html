Bobby

Bobby maps what's really happening on the streets where you live. Neighbours report what they see, other neighbours confirm it, and the result is a map of one place at a time — not a national feed of somebody else's bad news.

Bobby is not an emergency service. Always call 999 in an emergency, or 101 to report a crime to the police. Reporting something on Bobby does not report it to anyone in authority.

Where Bobby is live

  • Kensington & Chelsea — Notting Hill to World's End
  • Hammersmith & Fulham — College Park to Sands End
  • Piddington & district — Piddington and the villages around it, in Oxfordshire

One area at a time, and it is the one you picked. A feed holding every place at once would be incoherent for everybody in it. If Bobby is not where you are, you can put your postcode district on the waiting list from inside the app — that is all that gets stored, and you are told when it opens.

How it works

  • Report in under a minute. What happened, where, a sentence of detail, and a photo or a clip if you have one.
  • Every report is screened before it appears. An automated check first, a human for anything it is unsure about.
  • Neighbours confirm. Enough independent confirmations mark a report verified. How many depends on how big the area is — three in a London borough is a fair test, and in a village of a few hundred people it is an impossible one.
  • Recorded crime, alongside. The map can also show Home Office street-level data for your area. It runs about two months behind and every screen that shows it says so.

Reporting incidents, not people

Bobby exists to describe what happened where. It is not for saying who somebody is. Do not post names, phone numbers, email addresses, social media handles, full postcodes or home addresses — anything a stranger could use to find or contact a person. Reports that do are removed.

Photographs of what happened are fine, faces and number plates included. A picture of a van being loaded with someone's bike, plate and all, is the most useful thing this app carries. What turns a report into a risk is a name and an address, not a photograph. See the community rules.
