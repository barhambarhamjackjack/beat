Privacy policy

Last updated 30 August 2026

Bobby is designed to need as little about you as possible, and to show even less. You sign in so that contributions can be held to account; nobody using Bobby is ever told who wrote what.

Who is responsible for your data

The data controller is Jack Barham, a sole trader based in the United Kingdom, contactable at jack@barhamfamily.com. This policy is written to meet the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018.

About background location, plainly

Nearby alerts are the one feature that can look at where you are when Bobby is closed, so here is exactly what it does. It is off unless you turn it on. It uses iOS significant-change monitoring rather than continuous GPS, so it wakes when you have moved a few hundred metres, using radios your phone is already listening to. Your position is rounded to about 110 metres before it leaves your phone, and rounded again when it arrives. Only the most recent one is kept — there is no history, and no track. Nothing is sent if you have not moved. Switching it off deletes the stored position rather than merely stopping the updates, and a stored position is deleted automatically after 14 days regardless.

What Bobby does not collect

  • No name (unless you choose a display handle), date of birth, phone number or postal address
  • No contacts, and no access to your photo library or camera except for the specific photo or clip you choose to attach to a report
  • No audio except the sound recorded as part of a video you attach; Bobby never records audio on its own
  • No advertising identifier, and no tracking across other companies' apps or websites
  • No analytics SDKs and no third-party advertising
  • No continuous location tracking, and no location history — see above

What is public

Once it is published, a report's category, title, description, area, coordinates and any attached media are visible to everyone using Bobby. Your identity is not attached and is never shown — not your name, not your email, and not an account identifier. Other people cannot tell which reports came from the same person; the app is never sent that information. Coordinates are rounded to a privacy grid before they are stored — about 56 metres in a London borough, about 250 metres in a village — so a pin describes a street, not a door.

If you choose a display handle it appears on your comments, and it is moderated before it is approved. Please treat anything you write as public and permanent, and do not include information that identifies you or anyone else.

Automated screening of what you post

Reports are screened automatically before they appear, to catch content that names or locates a private individual. Anything the screener is unsure about is held for a person to look at, and anything it rejects can be appealed by emailing us. The decision that matters — whether your report is published — is never taken by the automated step alone in a case it is not confident about.

Who your data is shared with

Bobby runs on Supabase (database, storage and hosting) and uses Expo's push service to deliver notifications. Map tiles are served by OpenFreeMap using OpenStreetMap data; requesting a map tile discloses your IP address to them in the ordinary course of loading a map. Recorded-crime data is fetched from the Home Office's data.police.uk, which discloses your IP address to that service and the outline of the area you are looking at — never your own position. Automated screening of report text is carried out by Anthropic. Bobby does not sell data, and shares nothing for advertising.

Bobby may disclose information where legally required, for example in response to a valid court order or a police request that meets the statutory conditions.

How long data is kept

  • Published reports — kept indefinitely as part of the public record of the map, unless you delete them or ask for erasure
  • Rejected or removed reports — kept for 90 days for moderation audit, then deleted
  • A stored position for nearby alerts — the latest one only, deleted when you switch the feature off, when you sign out, or automatically after 14 days
  • Waiting list postcode districts — until Bobby launches in that area, or you ask us to remove yours
  • Push tokens — until you disable notifications, sign out, or delete your account
  • Your account and the Apple refresh token — until you delete your account, which erases both immediately and revokes Bobby's access to your Apple ID

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you may request access to your data, correction, erasure, restriction of processing, or portability, and you may object to processing based on legitimate interests. You can withdraw consent for location or notifications at any time in iOS Settings, and for nearby alerts inside the app.

To exercise any of these, use the in-app route: open Bobby, go to You → Delete my data. It deletes your account and everything tied to it immediately, with no email required.

You can also email jack@barhamfamily.com. Requests are answered within one month, as the law requires. See Delete my data for detail.

If you are unhappy with how your data has been handled, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk or on 0303 123 1113.

Children

Bobby is not directed at children and is age-rated accordingly on the App Store. It is not knowingly used to collect data from anyone under 13. If you believe a child has submitted personal data, contact us and it will be deleted.

Security

Data is transmitted over HTTPS and stored in an access-controlled database with row-level security, so a device can only read published reports plus its own pending ones, and authorship is never sent to any client. No system is perfectly secure; please do not put sensitive information in a report.

Changes

Material changes to this policy will be announced in the app before they take effect. The date at the top always reflects the current version.
